Volunteer Guide

How to Volunteer

  1. Explore the map Browse the Project Map and use the filters to find projects that match your interests, skills, language, and preferred duration of stay.
  2. Read the project details Click on a pin to open the project card. Review the organisation, location, sport, target group, and required skills to assess whether the opportunity is a good fit for you.
  3. Visit the organisation's website Click Visit organisation website in the project card to access detailed information about the volunteer programme directly from the organisation.
  4. Get in touch Contact the organisation through their official channels. Express your interest and ask about the application process, availability, and any prerequisites.
  5. Prepare your application Most organisations ask for a motivation letter and a CV. Highlight relevant experience — coaching, teaching, community work — and explain why you are drawn to this specific project.
  6. Get ready to go Once accepted, arrange your travel documents, visa, and travel insurance. Reach out to the organisation for any on-boarding information or pre-departure preparation they recommend.

Frequently Asked Questions

Not at all. Many Sport for Development projects welcome volunteers with no prior sports expertise. What matters most is a genuine motivation to contribute, an openness to other cultures, and a willingness to learn. Some projects do require specific skills — such as coaching or teaching — but these are clearly indicated in the project details on the map.

Durations vary widely depending on the organisation and programme. Some projects offer short-term stays of one to three weeks, while others expect a commitment of several months or up to one year. You can filter projects by duration on the map to find opportunities that match your availability.

Most Sport for Development volunteer positions are unpaid. Volunteers typically cover their own travel and living expenses, though some organisations provide accommodation, meals, or a small stipend. We recommend checking directly with the organisation about what is included before committing. Some positions may also be eligible for funding through national volunteer programmes or grants.

English is the working language for many international S4D projects, but some programmes operate in French, Spanish, Arabic, or Portuguese. The language requirements for each project are listed in the project details on the map. Even if the local language differs, a basic knowledge of it is always appreciated and can greatly enrich your experience.

Yes. Many S4D organisations actively welcome students, particularly those studying sport science, education, public health, international development, or social work. A volunteer stay can be a valuable complement to academic training and may even qualify as an internship or fieldwork placement, depending on your programme. Check with your university and the organisation to explore this possibility.

All organisations featured on this map have been selected based on publicly available information and verifiable online presence. Before committing, we recommend researching the organisation independently: look for an official website, annual reports, and reviews from past volunteers. If a programme asks for large upfront fees without clear justification, treat this as a warning sign.

Field conditions vary greatly depending on the country and project. Some programmes are well-structured with clear tasks and supervision; others are more flexible and require you to show initiative. Expect to work in under-resourced environments, adapt to local customs, and sometimes face logistical challenges. The experience is rarely comparable to a typical job — but the personal and professional growth it offers is equally uncommon.

Most organisations require volunteers to be at least 18 years old, particularly for international placements. Some programmes designed for youth exchanges may accept participants from age 16 with parental consent. There is generally no upper age limit — experienced adults and retirees with relevant skills are often welcomed. Always check the specific requirements listed by the organisation you are interested in.

Travel insurance is strongly recommended and required by many organisations before you depart. Make sure your policy covers medical evacuation, trip cancellation, and liability. Vaccination requirements depend on the destination country — consult your local travel medicine clinic or government travel advisory well in advance, as some vaccines require several weeks to take effect. Carrying a basic first-aid kit and any personal medication is also advisable.

Good preparation makes a significant difference. Learn as much as possible about the country, its culture, and the local context of the project. If you have time, pick up some basics of the local language. Review the organisation's programme materials and ask your contact person any open questions before you leave. Mentally preparing for cultural differences and a degree of uncertainty will help you adapt quickly and make the most of your experience from day one.
